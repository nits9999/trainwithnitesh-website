Nutrition Guide

The nutrition basics that actually matter

You do not need a perfect diet — you need these fundamentals done consistently.

Protein First

Anchor every meal around a protein source. It keeps you full, protects muscle during fat loss, and supports growth during a surplus.

Whole Foods, Mostly

Build meals around foods close to their natural form — dal, rice, roti, vegetables, eggs, paneer, chicken — and treat packaged food as the exception.

Consistent Meal Timing

Eating at roughly the same times each day helps regulate hunger and makes it far easier to stay on track long-term.

Hydration & Fibre

Water and fibre-rich vegetables are the most underrated fat-loss tools — they manage hunger without adding significant calories.

Track for Awareness, Not Obsession

Logging food for a few weeks builds awareness of portions and habits — it is a learning tool, not a permanent requirement.

Plan for Real Life

A nutrition plan that cannot survive a wedding, a work trip or a cheat meal is not a plan — flexibility is what makes results stick.

Supplements Guide

What actually helps — and what's optional

Supplements support a good diet and training program — they never replace one.

Whey Protein

A convenient way to hit your daily protein target when whole food alone is difficult to fit in, especially around training.

Creatine Monohydrate

One of the most researched supplements for strength and training performance. Simple, well-studied, and widely used.

Multivitamin

A general safety net for micronutrient gaps, particularly useful when your diet is inconsistent or restrictive.

Omega-3

Commonly used to support general health, especially for those who don't regularly eat fish.

This is general educational information, not individual medical or dosing advice. Needs vary by person — talk to Nitesh or a healthcare professional before starting any new supplement, especially if you have an existing health condition.

Latest Articles

Straight talk on fitness & nutrition

Short, practical reads — no fluff, no fad-diet nonsense.

Fat Loss

5 Fat Loss Mistakes Beginners Make

The most common ways a fat loss plan quietly stalls — and how to fix each one.

1. Cutting calories too aggressively, which tanks energy and adherence. 2. Ignoring protein intake, which speeds up muscle loss along with fat. 3. Doing endless cardio while under-eating instead of pairing moderate cardio with strength training. 4. Weighing daily and reacting to normal water-weight swings instead of tracking weekly trends. 5. Treating fat loss as a sprint instead of a 10–16 week process with a realistic, sustainable deficit.

Nutrition

How Much Protein Do You Really Need?

Cutting through the noise on daily protein targets for fat loss and muscle gain.

For most active individuals, 1.6–2.2g of protein per kg of bodyweight covers muscle repair and growth needs, whether you're cutting or building. Beyond that range, extra protein mostly just adds calories without added benefit. Spreading intake across 3–4 meals a day, with a source at each meal, matters more than hitting an exact number down to the gram.

Training

Why Progressive Overload Is Non-Negotiable

The single training principle that separates real progress from spinning your wheels.

Your body only adapts when it's asked to do slightly more than it's used to — more weight, more reps, or better technique over time. Repeating the exact same workout indefinitely is why progress stalls after the first few months. Tracking your lifts and aiming to beat them, even marginally, week over week is what keeps results moving.

Mindset

Consistency Beats Intensity, Every Time

Why a mediocre plan followed consistently outperforms a perfect plan followed sporadically.

A 70%-effective plan followed 90% of the time will always outperform a 100%-perfect plan followed 40% of the time. The clients who see the best results aren't the ones with flawless discipline — they're the ones who show up on the average, unmotivated days too. Building a routine you can actually sustain matters more than chasing an optimal one you'll abandon in three weeks.

Recovery

Sleep: The Recovery Tool Nobody Tracks

Why poor sleep can quietly undo good training and nutrition work.

Sleep is when most muscle repair and hormone regulation happens. Consistently getting under 6 hours a night is linked to worse recovery, higher hunger levels, and reduced training performance — regardless of how dialled-in your diet and workouts are. Treating sleep as part of the program, not an afterthought, is often the missing piece for stalled progress.

Muscle Gain

Bulking Without Getting Overly Fat

How to gain muscle in a surplus without the excess fat gain that usually comes with it.

A slow, controlled surplus of roughly 200–300 calories above maintenance is enough to support muscle growth without piling on unnecessary fat. Pairing this with progressive strength training and adequate protein means more of the weight gained is muscle. Tracking weekly weight trends and adjusting calories when gain speeds up too fast keeps the process controlled.
